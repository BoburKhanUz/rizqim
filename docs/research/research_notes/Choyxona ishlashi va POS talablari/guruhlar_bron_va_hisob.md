# Choyxona customers, groups, bookings and bill-splitting in Uzbekistan: input for POS design (bookings, recurring groups, deposits, split bills, nasiya)

Scope and method:
- About 27 searches and fetches in Uzbek (Latin), Russian and English, done October 2026.
- This builds on `Ovqatlanish formatlari POS talablari/choyxona_osh_milliy.md` and does not repeat its findings: The Choyxona kabinkas for 8–22 people, the 12% service charge at the Plov Center, osh prices, and the Palov indeksi.

Labels used in these notes:
- **[ESKI]** marks sources from before 2024. Ethnographic background is often older and is kept, but marked.
- **[XULOSA]** marks my inference, not a sourced fact.
- Diaspora sources were excluded from the findings. For example, Amerika Ovozi on gashtak among Uzbeks in the USA ([amerikaovozi.com, 2019](https://www.amerikaovozi.com/a/uzbeks-in-america-gashtak/4834909.html)) describes monthly rotating hosting, but it is outside Uzbekistan.

General caveat: very little public, operational material exists on how a normal (mahalla) choyxona takes bookings, deposits or nasiya. Choyxonas run their bookings through phone and Instagram/Telegram DMs, and those are not indexable. The strongest operator-level source is one 2023 interview with the founder of The Choyxona (Tashkent). Many points below are therefore gaps to validate in owner interviews.

## 1. Group types and occasions (gap/gashtak, ziyofat, birthdays, erkaklar oshi, nahor oshi, Thursday/Friday osh, korporativ, family, wedding-related, mahalla, iftar)

### Takeaway
Documented occasions for a choyxona visit:
- gap/gashtak (a regular same-sex circle of friends, classmates, neighbours or colleagues)
- young men's parties ("yigitlar bazmi")
- teenagers' birthdays, often with parents
- religious and seasonal holidays
- wedding and funeral-related nahor oshi
- Ramadan iftar (the new-format choyxona gives a free iftar set with evening orders)

Demand peaks Thursday–Saturday evenings. The traditional choyxona is a male space. Women's gaps go to cafes and restaurants instead.

### Cited Findings
- **Who books The Choyxona (Tashkent):**
  - Groups come "yigitlar bazmini o'tkazish uchun" (young men's parties) or "'gap' o'ynash uchun" (to play gap).
  - Others come to cook themselves in a kitchen-equipped kabina.
  - The audience used to be 35+. Now 15–16-year-olds also come, often with parents and frequently for birthdays.
  - "Payshanba, juma va shanba kunlari deyarli har doim odamlar liq to'la."
  - 190 seats. Weekdays bring about 130–140 guests.

  — [Spot.uz, 3 Mar 2023](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/) [ESKI, but the only operator source]
- **Gap is a regular, single-sex gathering:**
  - It is a regular get-together of people of similar age, typically 3–12 people and sometimes 30+.
  - Members are linked by friendship, family, school, neighbourhood or work.
  - It was historically male only. Women's gaps now exist, but gaps "still tend to be single-sex".

  — [Global Informality Project, "Gap (Uzbekistan)", T. Alexandrov, page last edited 7 Jan 2020](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Gap_%28Uzbekistan%29) [ESKI; fieldwork 2013–14 per [Alexandrov 2017, Europe-Asia Studies](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09668136.2017.1355965)]
- **Gender and venue:**
  - The chaikhana is described as exclusively male, so women do not gather there.
  - Women's gaps go to homes, cafes or restaurants.
  - Men's gatherings are held without a fixed occasion or for Navruz, Kurban-bayram and Uraza-bayram, weddings and birthdays.

  — [Russian Wikipedia, "Гаштак"](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%88%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BA) (unreviewed version, sources are Tajik and Soviet ethnography; treat as background)
- **Older accounts of the choyxona as a male space:**
  - A Tashkent memoir blog: "закрытое для посещения женщинами" (closed to women); men meet friends there for plov. Women increasingly hold their gatherings in "кафе или ресторан". — [mytashkent.uz, 2006](https://mytashkent.uz/2006/09/04/gap/) [ESKI]
  - Svoboda: "закрытое для посещения женщинами" (closed to women). — [Radio Svoboda, 17 Dec 2006](https://www.svoboda.org/a/367712.html) [ESKI]
- **Winter gashtak in the south:**
  - In Surxondaryo and Qashqadaryo, gashtak is also called "o'tirish". It is a winter custom, and men and women meet separately.
  - On the men's side it is held "mahalladagi choyxona yoki biror xonadonda" (in the mahalla choyxona or someone's home).

  — search summary of [in-academy.uz folklore article](https://in-academy.uz/index.php/ejsspc/article/download/24256/15931/23132) and [inlibrary.uz](https://inlibrary.uz/index.php/science-research/article/view/58760) (not fetched in full)
- **Nahor oshi (morning osh):**
  - A ritual at weddings and funeral gatherings (to'y, aza marosimlari). The day before, men hold "sabzi to'g'rash" (carrot cutting). — [uz.wikipedia, Nahor oshi](https://uz.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahor_oshi)
  - Service order: non and tea first, then osh, served one plate per two guests ("ikki kishiga bir lagan"). Only men serve at to'y oshi and ma'raka oshi. — [meros.uz, "Nahorgi osh"](https://meros.uz/object/nahorgi-osh) (search summary; undated)
- **Iftar 2025:**
  - The Choyxona was the only choyxona in Afisha's Tashkent iftar list. It gives a complimentary set with every evening order.
  - Restaurant iftar sets ranged from about 99,000 to 600,000 UZS. Some are priced "for two" or "for four" (Prime 595,000 for four; Shashleek 1,100,000 for four).

  — [Afisha.uz, 4 Mar 2025](https://www.afisha.uz/ru/gorod/2025/03/04/iftar-2025)
- **Iftar 2026:** ANONS.uz listed iftar sets in Tashkent again (e.g. Kaspiyka 255,000–295,000 per set). — [ANONS.uz 2026](https://www.anons.uz/ru/digest/zavedeniya-predlagayushchie-iftar-sety-v-tashkente-2026) (search summary only)
- **Corporate and other events:** The Choyxona markets banquets, closed corporate events, children's birthdays and presentations. — [Afisha.uz, 2022](https://www.afisha.uz/ru/gorod/2022/06/11/the-choyxona) [ESKI]

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] The occasion set implies an "event type" tag on bookings:
  - gap
  - tug'ilgan kun
  - ziyofat/bazm
  - korporativ
  - nahor oshi / ma'raka
  - iftor
  - oilaviy
- An event-type tag lets the owner forecast load (Thu–Sat evenings, Ramadan evenings, wedding-season mornings) and drives the menu template.
- [XULOSA] Mahalla choyxonas may get nahor oshi and ma'raka orders as bulk osh, by kazan or by guest count. These are often off-premise or for a large hall, so they behave like catering orders, not table orders.
- [XULOSA] Ramadan needs time-boxed bookings. Everyone sits at maghrib, so there is a hard start time and short dwell, and an iftar-set item priced per person or per table.

### Gaps
- No source on "erkaklar oshi", "payshanba oshi" or "juma oshi" as booked group events at choyxonas (search returned nothing specific).
- No source on mahalla-committee events at choyxonas.
- No source on korporativ share at choyxonas, or on family (mixed-gender) visits to new-format choyxonas beyond the teen-birthday mention.

## 2. How gap/gashtak works financially; how the choyxona treats a regular group

### Takeaway
Three financial models are documented, and they can be combined:
1. **Equal share per meeting:** everyone pays an equal share of food and venue costs at the end of the meal.
2. **Host pays:** the host of the turn pays for the meal (navbat bilan mehmondorchilik).
3. **Savings pot (ROSCA, "gap o'ynash" / "gap o'yini"):** each member gives an agreed sum every meeting, and the person whose turn it is takes the whole pot. In some versions the pot taker also hosts at their own expense.

A leader (jo'ra boshi) keeps the fund and the schedule. Meetings are weekly or monthly. I found no source on choyxonas giving regular groups discounts, monthly settlement or credit.

### Cited Findings
- **Alexandrov / Global Informality Project** [ESKI, fieldwork 2013–14] ([source](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Gap_%28Uzbekistan%29)):
  - **Venue and cooking:** meetings are at a member's home or at a cafe, restaurant or chaikhona. At the chaikhona, members "can rent cooking utensils" and cook plov themselves.
  - **Paying for the meal:** "usually everyone contributes an equal share at the end of dinner to cover food and rental costs". Alternatively, members take turns hosting and paying.
  - **Savings gap ("igrat' v gap"):** each member hands the host an agreed amount at each dinner. A pre-agreed rotation list decides who receives the lump sum.
  - **Contribution size:** about 50,000–500,000 UZS per dinner (then about US$19–190), up to 1,000,000 UZS among wealthy businessmen. These are 2013–14 amounts and inflation since then is large.
  - **Leader and obligations:** a jo'ra boshi organises the meetings and can be replaced if equality breaks down. Members also owe each other labour (hashar).
  - **Women's gaps:** contributions cover dinner, and any surplus goes to the hostess.
- **Recipient also hosts (2025):** A January 2025 fatwa Q&A describes the current practice:
  - Members meet monthly and draw lots for who receives the pooled sum, which each member gives as a loan.
  - The pot rotates monthly, and "the recipient is also expected to host the group at their own expense".
  - It is called "gap o'yini" in some cities and "gashtak" in others.
  - The scholar (Yusuf Davron) rules it permissible if there is no music, alcohol or mixed-gender gathering.

  — [islomqa.com, Q&A No. 1073, 12 Jan 2025](https://islomqa.com/question/gap-oyini-yoki-gashtak-qilib-bergan-odam-mehmondorchilik-qilib-bersa-bu-ribo-bolib-qolmaydimi/)
- **Group of 12 (Tashkent):**
  - Meets monthly, so each member "подает гяп" (hosts) once a year. Priority goes to a member with a big life event (wedding, newborn).
  - The group agrees a contribution, and "общий конверт передается хозяину... очередной «игры»" (the common envelope goes to that turn's host).
  - The founder or jo'ra boshi keeps the common fund and tracks the schedule.

  — [mytashkent.uz, 2006](https://mytashkent.uz/2006/09/04/gap/) [ESKI]
- **Rotating kazan duty:** in an established men's company at a chaikhana, "дежурство у казана передается из недели в неделю" (duty at the kazan passes week to week), so groups meet weekly. The source does not say who pays. — [Radio Svoboda, 2006](https://www.svoboda.org/a/367712.html) [ESKI]
- **Equal chip-in:** all participants "скидываются на определенную сумму" (chip in a set amount) depending on food and venue rental. In women's gaps the money goes to the woman whose turn it is, and she chooses venue and dishes. — [ru.wikipedia, Гаштак](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%88%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BA)
- **Dictionary definitions:**
  - Gap-gashtak is a ziyofat held weekly or monthly at one of the friends' places, in turn.
  - In winter, friends collected money or produce and shared equally.

  — [uz.wiktionary, gap-gashtak](https://uz.wiktionary.org/wiki/gap-gashtak); [izoh.uz, gashtak](https://izoh.uz/word/gashtak) (search summaries)
- **"Gap" at a modern choyxona:** The Choyxona names gap players as a customer type. Groups book cabins and stay about 5.5 hours. — [Spot.uz 2023](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/) [ESKI]
- **Source conflict on who pays:**
  - Alexandrov: equal share at the end of dinner is "usual".
  - islomqa and mytashkent: the pot recipient or host of the turn bears the hosting.
  - ru.wikipedia: pooled money goes to the turn-holder, who organises.

  These are probably different sub-types: an eating circle vs. a savings circle (ROSCA). They co-exist.

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] A POS "Doimiy guruh" (regular group) entity should hold:
  - name
  - jo'ra boshi (contact person and phone)
  - members list, optional
  - usual weekday and time
  - usual kabina
  - usual menu template
  - frequency (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly)
  - rotation list (navbat): whose turn it is this meeting
- With that entity, the POS can:
  - auto-create recurring reservations
  - pre-fill the "payer of the day" (navbatchi) on the bill
- [XULOSA] Payment modes to support on one group bill:
  - **Navbatchi pays all:** one payer, linked to the group member whose turn it is.
  - **Teng bo'lish:** equal split by N heads, including the service charge and kabina fee.
  - **Pooled cash from the jo'ra boshi:** one payer, but the POS should allow a note like "gap puli".
- [XULOSA] The ROSCA pot itself is member-to-member money and should not go through the choyxona POS. The POS only needs to record who paid the venue bill.
- [XULOSA] Self-cook gap ("kazan ijarasi" — renting utensils or kitchen and cooking plov yourselves) needs non-dish bill lines:
  - kazan/oshxona ijarasi
  - xizmat (staff)
  - possibly products bought by the choyxona at cost
- [XULOSA] A monthly group tab (settle at end of month) is plausible for long-standing regular groups at mahalla choyxonas, but it is unverified. Design it as a customer/group nasiya account (see section 5), not a separate feature.

### Gaps
- No source on discounts, fixed menus, or reserved weekly rooms that choyxonas give to regular gap groups.
- No 2024–2026 figures on gap contribution sizes or per-head spend.
- Alexandrov's full 2017 paper was not accessible (academia.edu returned 403).

## 3. Bookings: channels, lead time, deposit (zaklad/avans), cancellation and no-show, minimum order, peaks

### Takeaway
The single operator-level datapoint comes from The Choyxona (Tashkent, 2023):
- the venue "cannot operate without bookings"
- the deposit when booking is based on at least 250,000 UZS per seat
- most dishes must be pre-ordered with the booking, except quick kebab or kurtoba (about 30 min) and European dishes
- the off-the-shelf booking software was too rigid and had to be replaced

Booking is mostly by phone and Instagram. Cancellation and no-show rules were not found for any choyxona. Generic banquet practice is 30–50% prepayment, but that source is not Uzbek-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **The Choyxona founder interview** — [Spot.uz, 3 Mar 2023](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/) [ESKI]:
  - **Deposit:** "Joy band qilish paytida depozit miqdori har bir o'rindiqdan kamida 250 ming so'm olish kutilmalariga asoslangan." The deposit is sized on expected spend of at least 250k UZS per seat.
  - **Bookings are essential:** "biz joy band qilmasdan ishlay olmaymiz".
  - **Software:** "dasturiy ta'minotni almashtirishga to'g'ri keladi", because the system was not flexible enough for their settings.
  - **Pre-ordering:** most dishes are ordered in advance. Kebab or kurtoba (about 30 min) and European dishes can be ordered without pre-booking.
  - **Peaks:** Thursday–Saturday almost always full.
- **Booking channels listed by the press:** A July 2025 roundup of 7 Tashkent chaikhanas (TheChoyxona, Halva, Vostok, Chayhana No1, Tashkentskiy Dvorik, Boboy, Sultan Said) gives only Instagram handles as contact. It lists no booking rules, deposits or minimum orders. — [WeProject, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.weproject.media/articles/detail/chaykhana-v-tashkente-7-mest-gde-vy-smozhete-prochuvstvovat-uzbekskiy-kolorit/?amp=y)
- **Phone booking for iftar:** Afisha lists venue phone numbers for iftar bookings. — [Afisha.uz, 2023](https://www.afisha.uz/ru/restaurants/2023/03/29/iftar) [ESKI]
- **Same-day pre-order cutoffs exist:** Broccoli Park's iftar sets were available only by pre-order until 17:00 (as reported in search summary). — [WeProject iftar list](https://weproject.media/articles/detail/gde-provesti-iftar-v-tashkente-10-mest-so-spetsialnym-menyu-i-predlozheniyami/) (not fetched in full; the year is unclear)
- **Wedding banquets in Tashkent, 2026:**
  - The restaurant deposit is paid first, before other vendors.
  - Couples are advised to ask for the full sum first, because rental, service, textiles, equipment and extra hall time can be extra, and to fix refund terms in writing.
  - Example hall: 390,000–450,000 UZS per guest, rental from 25 million UZS, main hall at least 100 guests.
  - No deposit percentage is given.

  — [Afisha.uz, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.afisha.uz/ru/weddings/2026/08/24/wedding-budget)
- **Generic banquet practice (not Uzbek-specific):** prepayment 30–50% at signing, balance before or on the day; refund if cancelled N days ahead, as set in the contract. — [kb-horeca.site, banquets](https://kb-horeca.site/ru/restaurants/operations/banquets/) (Russian-language industry guide; geography unclear)
- **Kazakhstan comparison, outside scope:** a venue cannot keep the whole prepayment on cancellation, only proven expenses. — [finratings.kz](https://finratings.kz/news/17036-ostavili-predoplatu-za-uslugu-no-peredumali-kogda-obiazany-vernut-dengi/). I found no equivalent Uzbek ruling.

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Booking object fields:
  - date and time
  - kabina or zone
  - guests (planned vs actual)
  - event type
  - contact (name, phone, Telegram username)
  - source channel (telefon / Instagram / Telegram / walk-in / platform)
  - pre-ordered dishes (oldindan buyurtma: osh by kg or portions, kabob, special dishes)
  - deposit (amount, method: naqd/karta/Click/Payme/o'tkazma, received by whom)
  - minimum spend (per seat × seats, or per kabina)
  - status: so'rov → tasdiqlangan → avans olingan → keldi → yopildi / bekor / kelmadi
- [XULOSA] The deposit must be applied as a prepayment on the final bill, not as revenue at booking time, so that fiscal and cash reconciliation works. Forfeit on no-show should be a separate, explicit action needing manager rights.
- [XULOSA] Minimum spend per seat (the "250k per seat" logic) can be modelled as `min_spend_per_guest × planned_guests`. At close, the POS shows the shortfall, and the venue either charges it or waives it.
- [XULOSA] Pre-orders matter for the kitchen. Osh by kg and whole-lamb or tandir dishes need hours of lead time, so bookings must push a pre-order ticket to the kitchen (KDS) with a cook-by time.
- [XULOSA] The Choyxona's complaint about rigid booking software suggests requirements:
  - kabinas joinable for banquets
  - variable seat counts per kabina
  - long dwell time (about 5.5 h)
  - two seatings per day
  - free-text deposit rules
- [XULOSA] Phone and Instagram/Telegram DM are the de facto channels, so the POS should make manual entry fast, with a phone-number lookup that recalls the customer's or group's history.

### Gaps
- No published choyxona deposit %, cancellation deadline or no-show policy for 2024–2026, and no actual minimum-spend amount besides The Choyxona's 2023 figure.
- No data on booking lead times (how many days ahead) for kabinas or for nahor oshi.
- Instagram and Telegram posts of choyxonas (e.g. @thechoyxona, @chaihana.halva) could not be read with the tools available. This is the best place to collect real booking rules manually.

## 4. Bill-splitting customs (one payer / equal split / "har kim o'zinikini" / P2P transfers) and how the bill is presented

### Takeaway
The documented norms:
- In gap circles, either the host of the turn pays, or everyone pays an equal share at the end of dinner. Equality is a core value of the gap.
- Click (the dominant Uzbek payment app) has a built-in "Hodisa" feature to split a shared bill (explicitly "restoran hisobi") among participants and send each an invoice. One person can pay the venue and collect from friends by P2P.

I found no source on "har kim o'zinikini" (pay for your own items) at choyxonas, or on how choyxonas print group bills.

### Cited Findings
- **Gap meal payment:** "usually everyone contributes an equal share at the end of dinner", or members take turns hosting and paying. Equality is described as crucial to the group. — [Global Informality Project](https://www.in-formality.com/wiki/index.php?title=Gap_%28Uzbekistan%29) [ESKI]
- **ROSCA host:** in "gap o'yini" the pot recipient hosts at their own expense. — [islomqa.com, Jan 2025](https://islomqa.com/question/gap-oyini-yoki-gashtak-qilib-bergan-odam-mehmondorchilik-qilib-bersa-bu-ribo-bolib-qolmaydimi/)
- **Click "Hodisa":** "'Hodisa' xizmati foydalanuvchilarga umumiy hisobni bir nechta ishtirokchiga qulay tarzda bo'lish imkonini beradi."
  - The user creates an event, adds participants and enters the total.
  - The system divides it and sends invoices, and amounts can be adjusted per participant.
  - Typical uses named are gifts and restaurant bills.

  — [Click services page, published 10 May 2024](https://co.click.uz/xizmat-haqida/uslugi)
- **Hospitality norm:** sources describe hosting guests generously as a strong social norm, with shame in hosting poorly. They are general, not about paying at venues. — [centralasia.club](https://centralasia.club/uzbekistan/osobennosti-natsionalnyh-traditsij-i-obychaev); [Orexca](https://www.orexca.com/rus/uzbekistan/traditions.htm) (popular sources, weak)

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Likely the most common real flow in a choyxona group is "one payer at the table", for one of these reasons:
  - the host or birthday person pays
  - the navbatchi pays
  - the jo'ra boshi pays from collected cash
  - one friend pays by card and the others repay by Click/Payme transfer or "Hodisa"

  This matches the hosting culture, but I found no survey evidence.
- [XULOSA] Equal split (teng bo'lish) is the next most likely. Split by items is probably rarest for groups, though it may matter for young or mixed groups. POS priority: (1) single payer with mixed tender (part cash, part card, part QR), (2) equal split into N checks, (3) split by items or seats.
- [XULOSA] Show "per-head amount" (kishi boshiga) on the pre-bill (hisob/schyot). It helps groups chip in cash or set up Click Hodisa, even when one person pays.
- [XULOSA] Payment through several channels on one bill is likely common: cash plus Uzcard/Humo card plus Click/Payme QR. The POS must allow multiple partial tenders and partial payments over time (some members pay, then others), and keep the bill open until fully settled.

### Gaps
- No survey or article on how Uzbek friend groups split restaurant or choyxona bills in 2024–2026.
- No data on the share of "one person pays" vs equal split.
- No source on how choyxonas present the bill: hand-written vs printed pre-check, per-head line, service % line. The 12% service-charge-line fact is in the earlier note.

## 5. Credit / nasiya to regulars and groups

### Takeaway
Nasiya on paper ledgers ("qarz daftar") is described as extremely widespread in Uzbek small business. A vendor claims more than 1 million small businesses keep credit sales on paper. Several Uzbek digital nasiya-ledger apps exist (Daftaron, pDaftar, NasyaPing) with SMS or Telegram reminders and payment-term extensions. I found no source specific to choyxonas giving regulars or gap groups credit, nor default rates.

### Cited Findings
- **Daftaron (vendor claim):**
  - "O'zbekistonda 1 milliondan ortiq kichik biznes nasiya savdoni qog'oz daftarlarda yuritadi."
  - Debtors get automatic SMS or Telegram reminders when a debt falls due.
  - Tiers: Basic is limited to 70 credit entries.
  - It names pains with paper ledgers (lost or damaged records, miscalculations) but gives no default-rate figures.

  — [daftaron.uz](https://daftaron.uz/) (vendor marketing, not independent data)
- **Other nasiya-ledger apps:** NasyaPing allows extending a debt deadline by 30 or 60 days by agreement; pDaftar sends reminders. — [nasiyaping.biz](https://nasiyaping.biz/); [pdaftar.uz](https://pdaftar.uz/ru) (search summaries)
- **Open-source "Qarz daftar" POS module:** it does the following:
  - posts a debt sale to the customer balance, not to the cash register
  - stores partial payments as separate history entries
  - allocates payments FIFO to the oldest debt

  — [GitHub QARZ-DAFTAR](https://github.com/r2hdw65jg7-commits/QARZ-DAFTAR) (hobby project; design reference only)
- **Definition:** nasiya is a deferred-payment (credit) sale. — [izoh.uz, nasiya](https://izoh.uz/word/nasiya)

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Nasiya at choyxonas most likely appears in these forms:
  - **Mahalla regulars:** small daily tabs for tea, non and osh, written in the choyxonachi's notebook.
  - **Trusted regular groups or jo'ra boshi:** "pay next week / end of month".
  - **Mahalla/ma'raka organisers:** pay after the event.
- [XULOSA] POS requirements for nasiya:
  - customer or group account with phone number
  - "Nasiyaga yopish" (close on credit) as a tender type that does not hit the cash drawer
  - per-customer credit limit, due date and manager approval
  - partial repayments with history
  - an aging report
  - Telegram/SMS reminders, optional
- [XULOSA] Fraud controls are essential. Fake "nasiya" closures are a way for staff to hide cash. Each nasiya bill needs an identified customer (phone), manager approval and an owner alert, and nasiya must show separately in the shift Z-report.

### Gaps
- No choyxona-specific evidence on nasiya frequency, limits, or unpaid share.
- No independent statistic on unpaid nasiya in Uzbek catering. The 1M figure is a vendor claim about small business in general.

## 6. Typical group size, visit duration, and order sequence over time

### Takeaway
- **Gap groups:** typically 3–12 people (sometimes 30+). 12 is described as an ideal size for a monthly rotation.
- **Modern choyxona kabinas:** 8–22 seats (earlier note). A real example is 10 guests in a 16-seat kabina running up a 10 million UZS bill.
- **Visit length:** average time from opening to closing a bill at The Choyxona is 5.5 hours, giving about 2 table turns per day versus a 3–4 norm.
- **Order sequence:** non and tea come first, then the main dish (osh) once it is ready. Large events serve osh on shared plates (one lagan per two guests).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] The 5.5-hour dwell and multi-course flow mean a group bill is a long-lived open check with many rounds. The likely sequence is:
  1. dasturxon: non, tea, sweets, nuts, fruit, salads
  2. first hot dish or shashlik
  3. osh (often pre-ordered by kg or lagan)
  4. more tea and shashlik rounds

  The POS needs:
  - "add round" to an open check
  - kitchen tickets per round, with course timing (fire osh at a set time)
  - a running total visible to the jo'ra boshi on request (pre-check)
- [XULOSA] For capacity planning, each kabina realistically gets about 2 seatings per day (lunch, evening). A booking slot length default of 4–6 h is more realistic than restaurant-style 2 h.
- [XULOSA] Per-head spend at premium new-format choyxonas (about 250k minimum deposit basis; 1M/head in an extreme example) is far higher than a mahalla choyxona osh lunch (about 60–80k). Pricing and deposit rules must be configurable per venue.

### Gaps
- No observational data on average group size or dwell time for ordinary mahalla choyxonas (only the premium The Choyxona).
- No itemised group order data showing the real course sequence or shares (tea vs osh vs shashlik).

## 7. Booking platforms used in Uzbekistan for restaurants/choyxonas

### Takeaway
There is no dominant restaurant-booking platform in Uzbekistan. Options found:
- **Oshme:** a Telegram bot guide with a "reserve" button. Launched 2022, about 100+ venues in 2023, partnered with 2GIS, moved to SaaS.
- **Joyme (resttab.com):** an online booking and restaurant guide app. Details not retrievable.
- **RestoGuide:** a catalog that books via a Telegram bot.
- **2GIS cards:** cards for Tashkent venues show "Заказ столиков" / "Забронировать столик". In Russia that button comes through paid 2GIS ad packages or integrations such as Restoplace.
- **Single-venue apps:** for example the "Tashkent Restaurant" app with booking and loyalty.
- **Local vendors:** they sell Telegram or Instagram AI booking bots (from about $339).

Choyxonas themselves appear to rely on phone and Instagram/Telegram DMs. Searches for "Restolink" and "Bron.uz" were not productive.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] There is no incumbent booking platform to integrate with. The POS's own booking book plus a Telegram booking bot or Mini App is the realistic channel. That fits Rizqim's Telegram-first architecture.
- [XULOSA] Optionally, accept deposit online through Click/Payme/Uzum invoice links and auto-attach it to the reservation.
- [XULOSA] A "share booking link" for the jo'ra boshi to forward to the group chat, with date, kabina, deposit status and pre-order, would match how groups coordinate in Telegram. This is an inference, not observed.
- [XULOSA] Keep a 2GIS deep-link or phone CTA; 2GIS is the main discovery map in Uzbekistan. Treat 2GIS booking-button integration as low priority until its Uzbek availability is confirmed.
