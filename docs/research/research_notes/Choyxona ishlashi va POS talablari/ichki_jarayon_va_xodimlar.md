# How a choyxona runs inside: guest visit, working day, staff roles and money/stock leaks (Uzbekistan, 2024–2026)

Scope and method:
- About 25 searches and fetches (October 2026) in Uzbek (Latin), Russian and English.
- Sources covered: Telegram job channels (Xorazm_ish, ishbor, Ish_Toshkent, ishjizzax, buxoroda_ish), ishkop.uz, OLX, spot.uz, Raqobat qo'mitasi coverage and travelask.

This extends `Ovqatlanish formatlari POS talablari/choyxona_osh_milliy.md`. Facts already covered there (12% at Plov Center, Palov indeksi, the 2023 Afisha write-up of The Choyxona, buxgalter.uz legality) are not repeated, except where a new fact contradicts or extends them.

How to read this file:
- **[XULOSA]** marks my inference, not a sourced fact.
- **[ESKI]** marks a source from before 2024.
- **[ZAIF]** marks a weak source: a search-engine snippet not confirmed by fetching the page, an anonymous forum post, or an undated Telegram post.

Overall honesty note: public web sources almost never describe internal choyxona workflow (slips, tea counting, cash-outs, theft). Job ads give solid evidence on roles, shifts and pay models. Everything about order flow, billing mechanics and leaks is mostly [XULOSA] and must be validated in owner and staff interviews.

## 1. Staff roles and duties; who takes orders, who handles money, who opens and closes the bill; headcount small vs large

Job ads confirm the role set:
- ofitsiant (male-only is common for choyxonas)
- shashlikchi / mangalchi
- "povr" / oshpaz who "knows choyxona dishes"
- milliy taomlar oshpazi

## 2. Order taking and tracking: verbal, daftar, slips, software; tea and non without kitchen ticket; additions over hours for one room

### Takeaway
No public source describes the order medium in choyxonas: daftar, slips or software. Two facts are sourced:
- One Urganch fast-food waiter ad requires "computer program" skills, which shows that software is used in some regional eateries.
- In a kabinka choyxona the bill stays open about 5.5 hours on average, so long-running room bills with many additions are the norm.

### Cited Findings
- The Choyxona (Tashkent): average time from opening to closing a bill is 5.5 hours. Tables turn about 2 times a day, against an industry norm of 3–4 according to the owner. [ESKI 2023] — [Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/)
- The Choyxona: each kazan is cooked for only one kabinka or one guest. Kabob and kurtoba should be pre-ordered and take about 30 minutes. Food is not stored or sold the next day. [ESKI 2023] — [Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/)
- Urganch "Paxtakor gamburg centr" waiter ad requires the ability to work in a computer program ("kompyuter dasturi"). Pay "Kunlik: 3 foiz". Undated. — [Xorazm ish Telegram](https://t.me/s/Xorazm_ish?after=30236)
- Tashkent osh venue (Plov Center): no printed menu; the waiter lists what is available. — see earlier note ([Eurasia.travel](https://eurasia.travel/inside-tashkents-plov-center/))

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Long room sessions mean the bill must behave as a **long-lived "room tab"**:
  - many order rounds over 3–6 hours
  - each round time-stamped and attributed to a waiter
  - items routed per station (mangal, kitchen/qozon, tandir, tea/bar)
- [XULOSA] Tea (choynak) and non are typically carried from a samovar/tea corner and a non basket with no kitchen ticket. This is the most likely place for "hisobsiz choy/non".
- POS model for tea/non [XULOSA]:
  - **Option (a):** a quick "+1 choynak / +N non" button per room with no station routing, counted as items.
  - **Option (b):** an auto-charge rule per guest or per room ("choy-non" per person), which removes the counting problem entirely.

  Ask owners which they use today.
- [XULOSA] Pre-orders by station:
  - Kabob/kurtoba (about 30 min) and osh (per-kazan, per-room cooking) need a **"pre-order with ready-time"** at booking.
  - Shashlik is batch-cooked per round.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of daftar/paper-slip practice or of which software small choyxonas use (r-keeper/iiko only seen in vendor advertorials; see earlier note).
- No source on how tea and non are counted or priced in choyxonas: per choynak, per person, or free.

## 3. Osh in a choyxona: per kg / per lagan / per person; group brings own products and pays cooking fee; group cooks itself

### Takeaway
Sourced facts:
- In a kabinka choyxona, osh is cooked in a separate kazan per room or guest, to order.
- Some guests reserve the kitchen and cook themselves.

No published price was found for any of these:
- a cooking fee for own-meat osh ("go'shtini o'zi olib keladi")
- kazan or kitchen rental
- osh per kg in Uzbek choyxonas

Per-kg osh pricing is visible only in CIS diaspora chaikhanas (Moscow, Shymkent, Bishkek), which confirms that kg / "N persons" kazan units are standard in the cuisine.

### Cited Findings

  - Bishkek: "Плов ташкентский (3,3 кг) (на 6-7 человек)". — [navat.kg](http://navat.kg/dostavka/catalog/plov)

  - The rise in osh price is driven by meat, oil, carrots and chickpeas. Reference recipe: 700 g meat, 1 kg carrot, 1 kg rice. — [Daryo.uz, 2 Jun 2025](https://daryo.uz/2025/06/02/ozbekistonda-osh-tayyorlash-qimmatlashib-bormoqda-yangi-narxlar-qanday/) [ZAIF: search summary only]

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Domain knowledge, unverified for Uzbek choyxonas in 2024–26. There are likely three osh modes, and the POS should model all three as distinct line types:

  | Mode | What it is | POS line |
  |---|---|---|
  | **A. Choyxona's osh** | Sold per portion, or per kazan sized in kg of rice ("3 kg guruchli osh") or by number of persons/lagan | Product with unit kg-guruch / lagan / porsiya; recipe-based stock deduction |
  | **B. "Mahsulot bizdan emas"** | Group brings meat/rice/carrots; choyxona's oshpaz cooks for a fee (per kg of rice or flat), plus oil, firewood/gas and kazan | Service item "osh damlash xizmati" with qty = kg of rice; no product stock deduction except oil/fuel if supplied |
  | **C. "O'zi pishiradi"** | Group rents kazan/ochoq/kitchen (often bundled with the kabinka) and cooks itself | Rental item "qozon/oshxona ijarasi" (flat or per hour), plus separately sold tea, non, salads and drinks |
- [XULOSA] Mode A per-kazan pricing is a pre-order at booking that is usually prepaid or covered by the deposit. The osh ustasi should see it on a cook queue with a target time.

### Gaps
- **Key gap:** no Uzbek price list or ad for "osh damlash xizmati", "qozon ijarasi" or choyxona osh per kg. The Uzbek query "choyxona qozon ijaraga o'zimiz osh damlaymiz" returned nothing relevant. ## 4. Shashlik/mangal workflow: ordering by skewer, cooking queue

### Takeaway
Shashlikchi/mangalchi is consistently a separate role from the waiter and the cook in choyxona ads. In at least one Urganch choyxona ad the shashlikchi is paid differently from the waiters' 100–120k so'm daily rate, which suggests piece-rate or a percentage. Shashlik is priced per skewer: 17,000–20,000 so'm in Tashkent in 2025, per the earlier note. No source describes the mangal queue.

### Cited Findings
- Urganch choyxona ad: waiters 100,000–120,000 so'm/day, "shashlikchilar"; the pay line does not apply to shashlikchi. Shift 11:00–00:00. Undated. — [Xorazm ish Telegram](https://t.me/s/Xorazm_ish?after=30236)
- Urganch restaurant "OLIMP GARDEN": oshpaz and mangalchi, 3–6 mln so'm/month plus bonuses. Undated. — [Xorazm ish Telegram](https://t.me/s/Xorazm_ish?after=30236)
- Bukhara: a shashlikchi plus a skewer worker (sixchi) from 250,000 so'm/day, about 3 years old. — [ZAIF/ESKI: search snippet only, no URL confirmed]
- Kabob/kurtoba in a kabinka choyxona need about 30 minutes and should be pre-ordered. [ESKI 2023] — [Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/)

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Order unit is "six" (skewer), often by meat type (qo'y, mol, jigar, tovuq, qiyma/lyulya). Waiters say "10 ta aralash" verbally. POS needs:
  - qty-per-skewer items with modifiers
  - a mangal station screen or printer
  - batch "rounds" per room
- [XULOSA] If the mangalchi is paid per skewer or by % of shashlik sales, then the **mangal station's own count is a second, independent record** of shashlik sold. Reconciling mangal output against billed skewers is a cheap anti-theft control: the waiter can't serve unbilled skewers without the mangalchi noticing, unless they collude.
- [XULOSA] Raw skewers are often prepared (marinated, strung) in the morning. Opening count of strung skewers minus closing count gives the skewers cooked, which reconciles against skewers billed.

### Gaps
- No source on shashlikchi pay basis (per skewer vs % vs daily), mangal queue practice, or skewer prep counts.

## 5. End of visit: how the bill is calculated and presented; payment methods; tips

### Takeaway
New in this pass:
- In Nov 2025 the Raqobat qo'mitasi said restaurants and cafes were adding **10–20%** service charge and some demanded **advance deposits**.
- The decree's list of covered venue types explicitly includes **choyxona**.
- Deposits are used: a 2023 report on a kabinka choyxona put the deposit at about 250k so'm per seat, and a 10-guest bill came to about 10 mln so'm.

Tips are optional. Forum users say the service % often does not reach the waiter.

### Cited Findings
- Raqobat qo'mitasi warning (21 Nov 2025). — [Spot.uz, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2025/11/21/restaurants-warned); [Raqobat qo'mitasi](https://raqobat.gov.uz/uz/raqobat-qomitasi-respublikamizdagi-barcha-turdagi-umumiy-ovqatlanish-korxonalarini-ogohlantiradi/)
  - Some venues add 10–20% "xizmat haqi" on top of menu prices.
  - Some demand an "oldindan depozit".
  - The seller may not demand payment for services not rendered or push extra services.
  - Full information on prices and the waiter-service fee must be given in advance.
  - Self-service options are recommended.
  - Venue types covered include "choyxona".
- The Choyxona (2023) [ESKI] — [Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/oz/2023/03/03/the-choyxona/)
  - Deposit required at booking, reported as at least 250 thousand so'm per seat. [ZAIF: from page summarisation; verify the exact wording]
  - Example: 10 guests in a 16-seat cabin produced a 10 mln so'm bill.
  - Record bill: 45 mln so'm for two cabins.
- Travelask answers, about 3 years old [ESKI/ZAIF] — [Travelask](https://travelask.ru/questions/4701093_uz_tas_chto-naschet-chaevyh-v-uzbekistane-obratili-vnimanie-chto-ve)
  - In Uzbek cafes and restaurants the service charge is included in the bill at "10-25%".
  - Tips are rare and voluntary.
  - "эти деньги, которые включены в счет официант не получит"
- Payment methods (cash, card terminal, P2P transfer to the owner's card, Click/Payme QR): no choyxona-specific source found in this pass. See the earlier note for the 1 Apr 2026 cashless rules and the Moscow-diaspora anecdote of a waiter insisting on cash or transfer.

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Bill structure to model, in this order:
  1. items (all rounds)
  2. room / joy fee or kabinka minimum, if any
  3. service % (configurable, disclosed; 0% for self-service zones)
  4. minus deposit (avans) already taken at booking
  5. = amount due

  Then payment is split across tenders: cash / Uzcard-Humo terminal / Click / Payme QR / P2P transfer to a card.
- [XULOSA] **P2P to the owner's or waiter's personal card is a key leak and fiscal risk.** If the POS records "P2P to card X" as a tender, the owner can reconcile it against their bank app. A transfer to a *waiter's* card is a red flag.
- [XULOSA] Deposits must be stored against the booking or room and auto-applied at close. Otherwise an unapplied deposit is either double-charged (customer complaint) or silently pocketed.

### Gaps
- No Uzbek choyxona source on how the bill is physically presented (handwritten slip vs printed check) or on the share of cash vs card vs P2P.
- No published xona/joy fee amounts.

## 6. Staff pay models: daily wage, % of service/sales, tips; how pay ties to the bill

### Takeaway
Ads show three pay models coexisting in 2024–26 regional and Tashkent eateries:
1. **A flat daily wage**, the dominant model for choyxona waiters and ishchilar: 70k–150k so'm/day in Khorezm and Bukhara, 150k–300k in Tashkent.
2. **A guaranteed base plus a percentage of the waiter's own sales**: 2–5% seen ("100.000 гарант +%", "150 000 + 2%", "4% от общей", "Kunlik: 3 foiz", "5% tizim").
3. **Monthly salary**: 3.0–4.5 mln so'm for a Tashkent choyxona waiter, 3–6 mln for cooks and mangalchi.

Tips are usually the waiter's ("чаевые ваши"). The bill's service % is generally *not* passed fully to the waiter.

  - The day-end report must show, per waiter, sales base × % + daily guarantee ("garant"), whichever applies.
  - Service charge should be configurable as either a "venue revenue" or a "staff pool" split.
  - Tips should be recorded separately from the service charge, especially non-cash tips via card or QR.
- [XULOSA] A %-of-own-sales pay model gives waiters an incentive to *record* sales, which partly counters under-recording. A flat daily wage gives no such incentive, so venues on flat wages are more exposed to "hisobsiz" sales.

### Gaps
- No choyxona-specific confirmation of how the service % is split between owner and waiters.
- The pay basis of shashlikchi and oshpaz (per skewer, per kazan, % of sales) is not documented.

## 7. Opening and closing the day: cash counting, owner control, theft and leak patterns, current controls

### Takeaway
Sourced:

- He fires caught staff and aims to make theft "technically impossible" by controlling purchasing, logistics, deliveries and bookings.
- He reconciles cash, balances and income/expenses daily through an impartial accountant.
- Kassir job ads define the till handover routine: accept in the morning, hand over in the evening.
- POS vendors in Uzbekistan sell real-time Telegram alerts on every cash operation (see earlier note), which signals owner demand.

No Uzbek source names specific choyxona theft schemes. The patterns below are [XULOSA].

  - A Russian bar-cashier took cash from customers without entering it in the till. — [v-kurse.ru](https://v-kurse.ru/article/v-chaykovskom-barmena-kassira-obvinili-v-prisvoenii-deneg-klientov)
  - An Uzbek bank cashier appropriated client deposits (Jizzax). — [newshub.uz](https://newshub.uz/archives/18670) [ESKI]

  No Uzbek choyxona court or press case was found.

### Inferences
- [XULOSA] Likely leak patterns in a choyxona, to validate in interviews:

  | # | Pattern | POS control |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | **Hisobsiz choy/non/salat:** items with no kitchen ticket, served and paid in cash, never recorded | Auto per-guest choy-non charge, or a mandatory tap before serving; owner sees tea and non count vs guest count |
  | 2 | **Short-billing a long room tab:** some rounds left off the final handwritten bill, waiter pockets the difference | Every round entered and time-stamped; bill printed only from the POS; edits after a pre-check need a PIN plus an alert |
  | 3 | **Cash taken at the table, bill closed as "card" or deleted** | Close only by the kassir role; void/delete after print needs an owner PIN plus a Telegram alert |
  | 4 | **P2P to a personal card** | Tender "P2P → card #" with an allowed card list |
  | 5 | **Mangal/kazan leakage:** extra skewers or osh portions served unbilled, or sold to takeaway | Skewer prep count vs billed; kazan yield (kg rice → portions) vs sold |
  | 6 | **Deposit not applied / pocketed** | Deposit as a ledger entry linked to the booking, auto-applied |
  | 7 | **Purchase padding:** bazaar purchases with inflated prices or quantities, no receipts | Cash-out with category, qty and price, plus a photo of the slip; price history per item |
  | 8 | **Service % removed for friends, or added on top for strangers** | % applied automatically; removal needs a PIN and is shown in reports |
- [XULOSA] Opening and closing day ritual to model:
  - **Open shift:** kassir accepts the till with an opening float (count by denomination), and the owner sees the opening amount.
  - **During the day:** cash-ins (sales, deposits) and cash-outs (bazaar purchases, staff advances "avans", daily wages paid from the till).
  - **Close shift:**
    - expected cash = opening + cash sales + deposits − cash-outs − wages paid
    - actual count, difference ("kamomad/ortiqcha")
    - per-waiter sales and %
    - card/QR/P2P totals to reconcile with bank or Click/Payme
    - report pushed to the owner's Telegram
  - Paying **daily wages from the till at close** is likely common, given that most ads pay "kunlik". The POS should record each payout as a cash-out tied to the employee.

### Gaps
- No owner interview quantifying losses, and no list of named schemes in Uzbek or Russian.
- No data on what share of choyxonas use any POS vs paper.
- No source on whether daily wages are paid from the till.

## Summary: step-by-step model of one kabinka visit and one working day ([XULOSA], all to be validated)

**Guest visit**
1. Booking by phone or Telegram. The admin or owner records the date, room, guest count, pre-orders (osh N kg / kabob / kurtoba) and deposit (cash or P2P).
2. Arrival. The waiter opens the room tab and the deposit links to it. Tea and non are served immediately (auto-charge or tap).
3. Round 1: salads, drinks, shashlik by skewer, routed to the mangal station.
4. Rounds 2…n over about 5 hours (sourced average 5.5 h, 2023): more tea, non and shashlik, then osh served from the room's kazan (mode A/B/C).
5. Pre-check: items + room fee/minimum + service % − deposit.
6. Payment: cash, card, QR or P2P, possibly mixed. The kassir or owner closes the bill. The tip is recorded separately.

**Working day**
1. Morning: bazaar purchase (cash-out), skewer prep count, kazan plan per bookings, till opened with a counted float.
2. Shifts run about 09:00/11:00 to 23:00/00:00 (sourced from ads).
3. Close: cash count vs expected, per-waiter sales and %, daily wages paid out, owner Telegram report.

Role notes for the model:
- Waiters' daily wage (70–300k so'm) or % (2–5%) depends on POS sales attribution.
- The shashlikchi often has a different pay basis from the waiters.
