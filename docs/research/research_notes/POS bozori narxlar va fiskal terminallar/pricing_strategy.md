# Restaurant POS SaaS pricing in emerging and CIS markets (as of October 2026): tier design, units of charge, add-ons, annual plans and trials

Research date: 24 Oct 2026. Every USD figure is converted at Central Bank of Russia rates for 18–20 Oct 2026 (cross-rates are listed in Q4). "Accessed Oct 2026" means the page was read on 24 Oct 2026. Most vendor pages do not show their own "last updated" date.

## Q1. Tier structures and feature gating by vendor

### Takeaway
Nearly every CIS/emerging-market vendor uses 3–4 good/better/best tiers per location. The cheapest tier is the counter-service POS with stock and reports. The middle tier adds table service: waiter app, KDS, modifiers, delivery. The top tier adds reservations, advanced access control, loyalty/CRM and franchise tools. Loyverse is the only true freemium player: POS, KDS, loyalty and multi-store are free, and the back office is paid. Table reservations sit in the top tier at Poster and are a separately priced add-on at Saby. For a choyxona-first product, that means booking/deposits is usually treated as a premium feature.

### Inferences
- **Choyxona-relevant gating line.** In every CIS/Turkish table-service product, the boundary between the cheapest and middle tier is **"counter only" vs "table service"**: waiter app + KDS + hall plan. This holds for Poster (Mini→Business), iiko (Корнер→Кафе), Saby (Базовый→Оптимальный) and Adisyo (Lite→Standart for KDS). A choyxona is inherently table/room service, so putting the waiter app behind the second tier would effectively make the second tier the entry tier for choyxonas.
- **Reservations/deposits are a premium signal.** Poster puts table reservations only in Pro, and Saby sells booking separately. Rizqim's booking + deposits (+ room price lines) is a natural top-tier or add-on feature. Credit accounts (nasiya) have **no direct analogue in any vendor's tier list** we found. Adisyo's "cari hesap" was not visible on the page. This could be Rizqim's differentiator rather than a gate.
- **Two approaches to delivery/online ordering.** Poster bundles delivery management in the middle tier and sells its delivery website as an add-on ($19/mo). iiko and Saby sell delivery as an add-on module. A Telegram ordering channel maps to the "own delivery website/app" add-on that every vendor charges for separately.
- **Free tiers need another monetisation engine.** Loyverse runs on back-office add-ons and hardware. Toast and Square run on payment processing. Kaspi runs on banking. In CIS markets with free fiscal/payment hardware (Kaspi), standalone POS vendors compete by integrating with the payments player, not by being free.

## Q2. Unit of charge, add-ons, setup fees, annual discounts, trials and dealer margins

### Takeaway
The dominant base unit in CIS is **per venue/location per month**, with 1 register included. Extra registers, waiter devices and KDS screens are charged per unit. Quick Resto is the main exception: it charges **per terminal**. Add-ons are typically priced at **15–100% of the base tier**: KDS around $12 (iiko) to $30 (Square/Lightspeed), the waiter app around $9/device (Poster), and loyalty, delivery sites and QR ordering $7–$60.
- **Annual discounts:** about 10% at Poster and **"2 months free" (16.7%)** at Loyverse and Adisyo.
- **Trials:** 14–15 days are standard.
- **Setup fees:** these are where CIS players (Saby, iiko partners) make a lot of money.
- **Poster's dealer margins:** 10–50% recurring.

### Inferences
- **The pattern Rizqim could copy:**
  - Base price per location with 1 cashier device included
  - Waiter devices charged per device in bundles (Poster: 3 for $27)
  - KDS as a cheap per-venue add-on or included in the table-service tier (iiko 1,000 ₽ ≈ $12)
  - Online/Telegram ordering as a separate add-on (Poster Site $19, iiko QR 5,000 ₽, Saby delivery website 15,000 ₽)
- **Annual discounts in this segment** range from 10% (Poster) to 20% (Billz). "2 months free" (16.7%) is the most common clear framing (Loyverse, Adisyo).
- **Trials:** 14–15 days, card-free, starting on the top tier (Poster's reverse trial). This is the CIS norm. It is long enough to cover two weekly cycles of a choyxona, including weekend banquets/osh events.
- **Setup fees:** Saby's large launch fees and iiko partners' implementation fees point to a "low subscription + paid onboarding" model in Russia. Self-serve vendors (Poster, Adisyo, Loyverse) charge none. For small choyxonas, a free or remote setup with an optional paid on-site install looks like the competitive default.
- **Fiscal integration:** a paid add-on in Turkey (Adisyo ₺565/mo). In Kazakhstan it is free through Kaspi. In Russia it is bundled in r_keeper Lite (54-FZ). How Uzbekistan's online-KKM/fiscal-module requirement is handled is a pricing decision (bundle vs add-on), covered by the parallel fiscal-terminal research.

### Gaps
- No official data on per-order or %-of-online-order fees charged by CIS POS vendors themselves. Only aggregator commissions (15–30% for Yandex Eda/Samokat) were found — [iResto](https://iresto.ru/blog/skolko-stoit-avtomatizaciya/). The Western example is Toast's processing-linked pricing.
- Margins for iiko, Saby and Quick Resto dealers: not public.
- Poster's extra register ($19) billing period was not stated on the page.

## Q3. What converts small, price-sensitive operators; churn and price-sensitivity evidence

### Takeaway
Hard churn or conversion data from CIS POS vendors are **not publicly available**. The evidence is indirect:
- Poster grew to tens of thousands of venues with a low flat price, a full-feature 15-day reverse trial and a recurring dealer channel.
- Loyverse built a large base (a claimed 1M+ registered businesses) on a free core.
- Kaspi made fiscal/payments hardware free in Kazakhstan, which resets the price anchor for basic POS there.

User reviews say add-ons get expensive outside the US. Taken together, this suggests a **low flat entry price with the essentials** (POS + waiter + KDS), plus clearly separated add-ons.

### Cited Findings
- Poster says it serves "more than 18,000 venues" on one page and "27,000+ active venues" on another. The developer page says it reached "23,000 venues in 100 countries" growing by "450 venues per month". ### Inferences
- **Freemium with paid add-ons vs a low flat price.** Loyverse-style freemium works when the vendor has a second revenue engine (payments, hardware) or global scale. In CIS the successful mid-market players (Poster, iiko Корнер, r_keeper Lite) use **low flat prices of about $20–45 per venue with a full-feature trial**, not free tiers.
- **Fit for Rizqim's choyxona market.** Rizqim's add-ons (Telegram ordering, deposits/booking) generate revenue tied to transactions, so a **hybrid** is plausible:
  - a low-cost core POS
  - Telegram ordering monetised per order or as a %, aligned with value delivered
  - booking/deposits as a top-tier feature
- **Free hardware resets the price anchor.** Kaspi's free fiscal register shows that in Central Asia, payments/banking players can drive the price of basic register functionality to zero. If Uzbek banks or payment apps (Click/Payme/Uzum) do the same, Rizqim's core POS price would face a similar anchor. Its paid value would then have to sit in table-service workflows (waiter app, KDS, rooms, nasiya, banquet deposits) that bank-provided registers lack.
- **Pricing shape from the ARPU estimate.** The very rough Poster ARPU (about $50–60) is well above its entry price ($26–29). This suggests small operators upgrade or buy add-ons once they are onboarded, which supports a "low entry, rich add-on" structure.

### Gaps
- No public churn rates, trial-to-paid conversion rates or price-elasticity studies were found for Poster, iiko, Quick Resto, Saby, Loyverse or any Central Asian POS vendor. Investor materials were not found for these private companies.
- No vc.ru / kursiv.kz / profit.kz articles with POS churn data surfaced in searches.
- No evidence was found on whether choyxona-type operators in KZ/KG pay for POS at all versus using Kaspi Касса only.
